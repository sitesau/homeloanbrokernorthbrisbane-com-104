When evaluating home loan brokers north brisbane options, the first question many borrowers ask is whether there is a cost to them. The standard model for accredited brokers is that there is no direct fee to the borrower. Instead, the broker is paid a commission by the lender when the loan settles. This commission is disclosed in writing in the Credit Quote and Credit Proposal Disclosure before the borrower commits to anything. This arrangement means borrowers can access professional advice and lender comparison without paying an upfront fee. No churn, no pushy sales.

It is important to note that the broker is accredited by bodies such as MFAA or FBAA. This accreditation ensures they adhere to strict standards when handling the credit advice and the application end to end. Because the broker is paid by the lender, their primary focus is on securing the best loan for the borrower, not on pushing a specific product for their own gain. Understanding settlement timelines

Timing is a major factor in choosing a broker. For a standard purchase, the process from accepted contract to settlement usually allows 30 to 45 days. This timeframe covers lender approval, formal documentation, and settlement coordination. For refinances, the timeline is longer, typically 4 to 8 weeks. The extra time is often due to the outgoing lender's discharge process, which must be completed before the new loan can settle. Understanding these ranges helps borrowers align their loan with their settlement date.

In North Brisbane, a practical borrower question is usually tied to a settlement date, a refinance deadline or a planned renovation split. A broker can help flag whether the chosen lender will fit the file given these dates. If the dates are tight, the contract or discharge timeline deserves attention before rate shopping. This ensures that the borrower does not chase a sharp advertised rate before knowing whether the chosen lender will fit the file.

Deposit requirements and guarantees

Deposit requirements are another key area where a broker adds value. Most lenders ask for at least 5 per cent genuine savings plus enough to cover lenders mortgage insurance. However, eligible first home buyers may access the federal First Home Guarantee. This scheme allows a 5 per cent deposit purchase without lenders mortgage insurance, subject to annual caps and income testing. A broker can help determine if a borrower qualifies for this guarantee and how it impacts the overall deposit strategy.

For investors, the more useful early question may be whether cash should stay flexible for offset or future costs rather than being committed in a way that weakens the wider structure. The guide also points borrowers toward the Queensland First Home Owner Grant. Rather than assuming how that grant will be counted, ask how the selected lender treats it within the deposit position and what evidence is needed before lodgement.
